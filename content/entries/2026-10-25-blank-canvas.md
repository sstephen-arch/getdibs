AI tools have made building cheap. I ran
[11.5 billion tokens through Claude Code](/essays/where-my-tokens-go/) in under
eight weeks, and I have skills set up for most of the heavy lifting. None of that
helps when I sit down with nothing to build. The blank canvas is still the hard
part.

What changed it for me was to stop hunting for ideas. Every time I went looking
for a good app idea, I ended up with something generic that I lost interest in
within a week. The ideas that stuck came from things that already bugged me,
made me laugh, or that I knew better than most people.

[Let's Touch Base](https://letstouchbase.app) is the clearest example. I've sat
through a lot of icebreakers, and most of them go the same way: a login nobody
remembers, a link IT blocks, and twenty minutes gone before anyone plays. I'm
also competitive, and I think office jargon is funny. So the app is 19 quick
games named after the phrases we all love to hate, like Quick Sync, Put a Pin in
It and AI Can't Take My Job, and every round ends with a mock performance review.
My day job shaped it too. One iPhone hosts. Players join from their phone's
browser with a QR code or a four-digit meeting ID, nobody creates an account,
and on Wi-Fi the game stays on the local network. That's the version I'd want
to approve if I were the one in IT getting the request.

That's when building stopped feeling like work. When the jokes, the game rules
and the network design all come from how I actually think, the product doesn't
feel forced. Problem solving and having fun turn into the same activity. If
you're stuck staring at an empty repo, skip the brainstorm. Start with whatever
annoys you at work.
